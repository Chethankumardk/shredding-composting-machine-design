# Engineering Results

This directory summarizes selected engineering results from the university team project **Design and Fabrication of a Shredding-Cum-Composting Machine for Kitchen Waste**.

## Design Basis

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---:|
| Waste considered | 30 kg |
| Organic-waste density | 285 kg/m³ |
| Drum diameter | 270 mm |
| Drum length used in design calculation | 450 mm |
| Drum volume | 0.025 m³ |
| Operating speed used in calculation | 120 rpm |

## Shaft and Drive Design

The project calculations produced:

- Minimum required shaft torque: **11 N·m**
- Calculated minimum shaft diameter: **9.7 mm**
- Selected standard shaft diameter: **15 mm**
- Calculated power requirement: **138.21 W**
- Equivalent calculated power: **0.19 HP**
- Selected motor: **0.25 HP, 1200 rpm**
- Gearbox reduction ratio: **1:10**

The selected shaft diameter therefore exceeded the calculated minimum diameter.

## Blade Concept Comparison

Three blade alternatives were investigated.

| Alternative | Blade concept | Maximum stress | Maximum displacement | Maximum contact pressure |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | Propeller-shaped | 51.94 MPa | 0.3213 mm | 51.12 MPa |
| 2 | Rectangular | 11.58 MPa | 0.08645 mm | 10.91 MPa |
| 3 | Helical | 8.88 MPa | 0.2045 mm | 5.145 MPa |

### Alternative 1 — Propeller Blade

The report describes this concept as producing comparatively high stress and contact pressure.

### Alternative 2 — Rectangular Blade

This concept reduced the reported stress and contact pressure compared with Alternative 1, but the report identifies uneven mixing as its major disadvantage.

### Alternative 3 — Helical Blade

The helical blade produced the lowest reported maximum stress and contact pressure of the three alternatives.

The report also states that the helical geometry supports even mixing and pushes the mixture toward the exit.

For these reasons, the helical configuration was the preferred concept in the documented design study.

## Scope

These values are results reported in the academic project documentation.

They should be interpreted as results from a university conceptual design, analysis, and fabrication project rather than as certified production-machine specifications.
