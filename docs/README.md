# Project Documentation

## Project Context

This repository documents a university mechanical-engineering team project focused on the **design and fabrication of a shredding-cum-composting machine for kitchen waste**.

The project followed a product-development workflow combining customer requirements, mechanical design, engineering calculations, component selection, structural analysis, and fabrication considerations.

## Engineering Workflow

The documented project workflow included:

1. Identification of customers and user needs
2. Customer feedback and survey
3. Ethnographic research
4. Quality Function Deployment (QFD)
5. Product Design Specification (PDS)
6. Composting-process definition
7. Mechanical design calculations
8. Component and mechanism development
9. Machine model design
10. Blade concept analysis
11. Cost estimation
12. Fabrication and project evaluation

## Mechanical System

The machine concept integrates several subsystems:

### Shredding System

Kitchen waste enters through the shredder, where the material is reduced before entering the mixing drum.

The documented drive arrangement uses a motor, gearbox, chain, and sprocket mechanism.

### Mixing Drum

The drum provides the chamber in which shredded waste and bulk material are mixed.

The design incorporates a shaft with helical blades for mixing and movement of material toward the outlet.

### Heating System

The documented concept uses a heating tank and heating element, with temperature control provided through a thermostat.

### Structural Frame and Enclosure

The mechanical components are supported by a structural frame and enclosed within the overall machine body.

## Mechanical Design Calculations

The project documentation includes calculations for:

- Drum volume
- Shaft torque
- Minimum shaft diameter
- Motor power requirement
- Motor selection

The calculation resulted in a minimum required torque of **11 N·m** and a calculated minimum shaft diameter of **9.7 mm**.

A standard **15 mm shaft** and **0.25 HP motor** were selected in the documented design.

## Blade Design Study

Three blade concepts were investigated:

1. Propeller-shaped blade
2. Rectangular blade
3. Helical blade

The documented analysis compares stress, displacement, reaction force, strain, and contact pressure.

The helical configuration produced the lowest reported maximum stress and contact pressure of the three alternatives and was also intended to provide mixing and axial movement of the material.
